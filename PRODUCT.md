# Product

## Register

product

## Users

General contractors, remodelers, and small-trade business owners running 1–10 person operations. They are multi-scene users; every JobFlex surface must survive all three:

- **On a jobsite, mid-task.** Glancing for the next move, pulling a number off a proposal, checking who's confirmed for tomorrow. Direct sunlight, gloves on or off, one-handed.
- **At a desk, end-of-day.** Cleaning up invoicing, drafting tomorrow's quotes, answering client emails. Quiet, longer sessions, denser data tolerated.
- **Standing in front of a homeowner.** Walking through an estimate, presenting a proposal, getting a signature on a phone. Must look credible. Cannot embarrass.

Primary job: run a small-shop contracting business from a single tool instead of stitched-together spreadsheets, paper, texts, and a half-used CRM.

## Product Purpose

JobFlex is the operating system small contractors actually want to use. It handles AI-assisted proposals, lead pipelines, scheduling, jobs, invoicing, and client portals so a one-truck owner-operator can run like a ten-person shop.

Success looks like contractors continuing to use it past the trial because the paperwork stopped being the thing they dread at 9pm. The current mobile redesign milestone exists because contractors live on their phones; the desktop-first version is the wrong shape for the dominant use case.

## Brand Personality

**Quiet, unhurried, workaday.**

The voice is "team that has run crews and lost sleep over unpaid invoices, now offering you the tool we built for ourselves" (verbatim sentiment from `src/app/(marketing)/about/page.tsx`). Confident through calm. Never breathless, never marketing-buzzy, never gimmicky.

The tool should feel like a well-kept shop: organized, a little beautiful, ready for the next job. Magazine-quarterly composure applied to a workbook.

## Anti-references

Four AI-slop traps to refuse on sight:

1. **Construction-app cliché** *(user-flagged as the most important to avoid)*. No hi-vis safety yellow, no hardhat icons, no hammer mascots, no blueprint backgrounds, no jobsite-photo hero, no orange CTAs.
2. **Generic SaaS-cream.** No HubSpot blue, no identical card grids of icon+heading+text, no hero-metric template tiles (big number + small label + gradient accent), no marketing-template chrome bolted onto product UI.
3. **AI-startup purple-gradient.** No purple-to-pink gradients on AI features, no sparkle icons on every button that touches AI, no glassmorphism by default, no Linear-clone dark.
4. **Consumer-cute.** No mascot illustrations, no playful pastels, no gamified streaks, no oversized emoji icons.

## Design Principles

1. **The well-kept shop.** Organized, a little beautiful, ready for the next job. Decoration earns its place or it's removed.
2. **Quiet competence over loud claims.** Show you've thought about the contractor's day; don't announce it. The interface argues with restraint, not exclamation points.
3. **Editorial over template.** Type and whitespace carry the weight. Hairlines beat full borders. Restrained color beats decorative color. Cards are usually the lazy answer.
4. **Multi-scene survival.** Every surface must read in daylight, present credibly to a homeowner, and not exhaust a contractor at 9pm. No choice optimized for only one scene.
5. **Practical AI, not feature-pageant AI.** AI is a tool inside the workflow (AI estimate, AI proposal draft), never a destination, never a hype surface. AI features earn their place through outcome, not chrome.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.2 AA** as baseline (contrast, focus, keyboard, semantic structure).
- **Outdoor-sunlight readability** treated as a hard constraint on mobile surfaces, on top of AA contrast minimums. Real contractors use this in glare.
- **Touch targets ≥44px** on mobile (locked in CLAUDE.md).
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` respected** throughout (already wired in Phase 0 primitives).
- **Light theme only** for this milestone. Dark mode is explicitly out of scope.

## Reference family

**Small-shop editorial.** The lane is Cabin, Sutro, Mast, early Stripe blog era. Generous type, hairline rules, paper-feeling surface, restrained color, occasional considered photography. Magazine-quarterly composure applied to a contractor's workbook. The locked tokens in `src/app/globals.css` already sit in this lane (Geist display, paper/ink palette, subtle grain, hairline borders); future design work extends the same lane rather than drifts toward Linear-clone product-minimalism or anti-SaaS opinionated.
